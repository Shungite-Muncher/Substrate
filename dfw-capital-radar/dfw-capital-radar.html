How it's scored

Each property earns points on four signals from its public HUD loan record. Points are added up and rescaled to 0–100. If a property is missing a field, that signal is left out and the score is rescaled over the rest, so a missing field never helps or hurts.

Score = (40 × Refi + 30 × Free + 30 × Hold + 25 × Equity) ÷ points available × 100

1 · Refi pressure (up to 40 of 100 pts)

Loan rate minus today's market rate (the slider). Full credit at 1.50 pts or more above market, scaled down to zero at or below market. These are refi leads for Marcus & Millichap Capital Corp.

2 · Free to transact (up to 30 pts)

Years since the loan's initial endorsement ÷ 10, capped at full credit after 10 years.

3 · Long hold / exit (up to 30 pts)

Years since endorsement ÷ 20 (capped), multiplied by a deal-size factor: the largest original loan amounts in the DFW set get 100% of that credit, the smallest 50%. An old loan on a big deal suggests a long-time owner who may be thinking about an exit.

4 · Equity (up to 25 pts)

(Appraisal-district value − HUD loan balance) ÷ value, from 0 (no equity) to full credit (no debt). Only scored where an appraisal-district match exists; everywhere else equity shows n/a and the score is rescaled over the other three signals. Equity = appraisal-district value minus HUD loan balance. Matched 94 of 160 Dallas/Tarrant loans on exact street number + street name against apartment-class parcels. Collin, Denton, Ellis, Johnson, Parker, Kaufman and Rockwall CADs not yet matched (n/a). Matches where the CAD value was under 25% of the loan balance (partial or under-construction assessments) were discarded. CAD values are tax-assessment values, often below what a buyer would pay.

“Why call now”

The line on each card comes from whichever signal earned that property the most points.

Assumption, not data: HUD loans usually carry a prepayment penalty that steps down over about 10 years. This page assumes 10% in year 1, falling 1 pt a year to 0% after year 10. The actual terms are in each loan's note and vary by deal (some have lockouts). Every “Est. prepay*” tag uses this assumption.
